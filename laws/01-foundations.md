# 01 · Foundations: Bauhaus and the Swiss grid, in plain words

Everything else in this repo is a modern restatement of ideas from two schools.

## Bauhaus (Germany, 1919 to 1933)

- **Form follows function.** Nothing decorative that does not do a job. Every rule, badge and line earns its place or it goes.
- **Primary geometry.** Square, circle, triangle as the base vocabulary. Clean rectangles and circles, no ornament.
- **Honest materials.** Show the thing as it is. Real product screens beat faked illustrations.
- **Asymmetric balance.** Weight balanced across the page without mirroring. A heavy headline on the left, a quiet block on the right.
- **Limited, purposeful color.** A small palette. One accent used for meaning, not mood.
- **Typography as structure.** Sans serif, few sizes, hierarchy by size and weight, text aligned to the grid.
- **Whitespace is a material.** Empty space is designed, not left over.

## Swiss grid (International Typographic Style, 1950s onward)

- **The grid is the law.** A modular grid governs every placement. Columns, gutters and a baseline rhythm.
- **Proportional spacing.** Distances relate to each other by ratio. Nothing is eyeballed.
- **Objective presentation.** The design serves the content. Clarity over cleverness.
- **Flush-left, ragged-right text.** Sans serif faces. Photography over illustration.

## Why this matters for web and AI-assisted builds

Design systems, 8px spacing scales, type ramps and semantic color tokens are these same ideas, typed into CSS. When an assistant guesses a margin, it is breaking the Swiss grid. When it adds a gradient blob, it is breaking Bauhaus. The laws that follow turn these principles into checks a machine can run.
